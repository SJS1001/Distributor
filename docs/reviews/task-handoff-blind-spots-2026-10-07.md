# Task handoff blind spots — 2026-10-07

The six entrances cover the main software journeys, but an entrance is not an operational completion contract. The largest shared blind spot is **who owns the next step, how soon they must act, who takes over when it stalls, and how the customer or next operator learns that action is needed**. Roles, scoped queues, statuses, history and recovery controls exist. A named handoff owner, approved response SLA, escalation route and proactive notification are not verified by this review; this is not a claim that the queues or authorization controls are missing.

This is a bounded source/document review of the working tree based on `eda07abbd2e75c57b1a2c6d053c4a3424c2aac7c`, including the current customer minimum changes. It performs no business writes, build, tests, deployment or provider activation. The [portal journey assessment](portal-journeys-2026-10-07.md) and its [release receipt](../evidence/PORTAL-JOURNEYS-2026-10-07.md) retain their own tested versions and synthetic/read-only limits. This review creates no new acceptance receipt and advances no task or product gate.

## Practical follow-up

Before operational acceptance, record for each handoff: a named responsible operator or staffed queue; the event that starts its deadline; the next expected action; completion evidence; escalation recipient and trigger; and how the customer/next operator receives an update. Agree these with actual enrollment, commercial, warehouse, warranty and finance operators, then observe representative complete cases and stalled exceptions. Do not invent SLA numbers from queue ages: [Operations health](../OPERATIONS-HEALTH.md) explicitly distinguishes creation age from time in state and does not establish an approved alert deadline.

Stripe/payment transport, QuickBooks and carrier access remain disabled/unqualified in the recorded pilot. Native invoice, cash/payment record, accounting preparation, carrier history and remedy controls must not be equated with successful external collection, posting or booking. Physical devices, actual stock/custody, representative-user completion and approved warranty/commercial policies still need evidence. Relevant acceptance work remains in [D-001/D-004/D-006, D-012–D-029, D-030–D-036 and D-040](../TASKS.md); status is governed by [CHECKPOINTS](../CHECKPOINTS.md), not this review.

## Additional operating checkpoints to make explicit

These are expected journey checks, not assertions that native controls are absent: before first ordering, staff/customer confirm billing and delivery addresses, tax treatment, account pricing, payment/credit terms, both minimums and appropriate user/site access. Before accepting an order, the buyer understands current stock/backorder status, freight/collection choice, quote expiry, total payable and the next action if approval or payment is outstanding. Catalog publication includes classification, commercial setup and customer visibility as well as media review.

Physical receiving needs a agreed procedure for supplier shortages/overages, damaged or wrong equipment, duplicate/missing serials, supplier returns and outstanding PO reconciliation. Fulfillment needs short-pick/partial-shipment communication, collection identity or carrier handover evidence, delivery exceptions and invoice/payment reconciliation. RMA closure should identify the actual remedy and financial/stock evidence, with a contact route for accessories, missing serials and disputed decisions. Each exception needs an assigned next action and visible status.
