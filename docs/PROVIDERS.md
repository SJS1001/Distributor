These are the planning candidates, not a market ranking or a promise of availability.

## Recovery and continuation

Sends move pending → running → completed, or unknown after lost responses. Each checkout/accounting send or reconciliation read acquires an exclusive durable token. Recovery invalidates tokens abandoned for two minutes and makes abandoned sends unknown; a stale sender/read cannot bind a result, overwrite an absence, or release its successor's claim. Recovery is scoped to the configured organization and also clears interrupted read claims. Legacy running intents without tokens still become unknown. Unknown operations are never automatically resent. Stripe lookup examines at most 1,000 sessions from the creation window; absence is inconclusive. QuickBooks accepts one matching invoice or cash payment; multiple results require review.

Current active finance/support grants and credential-change status are checked inside claim and completion transactions. Checkout/accounting queue commands recheck current grants, restore clearance and named customer consent before returning cached intents. Settlement requires current finance authority before and after retrieval, including inside its cash transaction; a restore hold prevents binding or cash application after I/O. Consent withdrawal during an already authorized call cannot recall its response: its qualified outcome may be retained, while subsequent outbound reads remain blocked.

Reviewed checkout closure and replacement are described in [the checkout runbook](CHECKOUT.md). Finance expires an exact current unpaid session under the shared claim with fresh authority/consent/restore checks immediately before writing. Only observed expired/unpaid sessions or unsent pending intents can gain a permanent reviewed successor. Original session callbacks retain their original invoice/money binding, and closure/replacement never creates native cash.

Callbacks move pending → processing → completed/waiting/blocked/failed. Waiting reads become due after 30 seconds; interrupted attempts become waiting after two minutes. Attempt numbers prevent abandoned workers overwriting successors. Residency/grant restrictions block verification; identity/money conflicts need finance review. UI/worker errors omit provider bodies, secrets and stack traces.

Operators use “Provider operations” and “Payment confirmations,” reconcile unknown sends and resolve causes before retrying verification. Synthetic tests cover lost responses, duplicates, stale claims and a new database connection. Actual termination during provider IO, external retries, rotation, prolonged outages/backlogs and alerting remain unverified.

## Credited cash refunds

Requests do not move stock or add a second credit. The Billing screen shows retained native requests and provider observations; manual payments require a separate unique bank repayment reference. Recording manual evidence is not independent proof of bank settlement.

For Stripe payments, finance queues one immutable refund intent, then sends the pending operation using its stable effect idempotency key. The test-only adapter retrieves the original settled PaymentIntent and verifies test mode, identity, received amount and currency before creating or reading a refund. The request sends only the payment identity, integer amount and internal effect/refund metadata. [Stripe creation documentation](https://docs.stripe.com/api/refunds/create) describes that interface; [refund objects](https://docs.stripe.com/api/refunds/object) have statuses `pending`, `requires_action`, `succeeded`, `failed` or `canceled` and do not themselves expose a live-mode flag.

Immediately after the preliminary PaymentIntent read and before SDK refund creation, the coordinator repeats current active finance/password authority, restore clearance, current named Stripe acceptance, its durable poll token and running/unbound operation checks. Billing verifies the frozen native intent, unknown reservation state and absence of prior provider proof through its own API. A changed restriction or abandoned claim blocks creation; the operation stays unknown and its reservation remains for qualified outcome reads. An absent lookup never permits resend. These checks narrow the race but cannot make native transactions and an external write atomic or recall a request already sent. Trusted custom adapters must invoke the supplied synchronous pre-write callback; the configured Stripe SDK adapter does so. The [refund write guard receipt](evidence/LOCAL-REFUND-PREWRITE-2026-10-01.md) records bounded mocked SDK timing and separate-connection checks, without new process-termination or actual provider qualification.

Observations remain immutable, including stale statuses ignored after success or a terminal failure. A later bank failure can reverse an earlier completed refund while retaining both observations. A returned transfer may instead become `requires_action`, removing confirmed refunded cash and reserving it again until correction succeeds or a terminal failure releases it, consistent with [Stripe refund handling](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds). This accommodates provider observations; additional payment methods are not qualified by it and current checkout creation remains card-only. Operators must reconcile the bank/customer outcome before any new repayment.

A lost send response remains unknown and is never automatically resent. “Check provider outcome” reads the original intent. A known refund is retrieved by its exact provider identity. No match proves neither failure nor safe resend. See [Stripe refund list pagination](https://docs.stripe.com/api/refunds/list). A new request/key must not be used to bypass unknown-outcome recovery.

## QuickBooks unapplied credit handoffs

After recording a native credit, finance can queue its QuickBooks handoff from Billing once the original invoice operation is completed. The command accepts only the native credit ID. Billing supplies original credited quantities, prices, tax and document totals; integration inherits customer/item/tax mappings and the permanent external invoice identity from the completed invoice intent. One immutable effect survives retries, restarts and competing command keys. Current finance authority, credential status, customer permission and restore clearance precede cached results. Support may send/read queued operations; buyers and commercial staff cannot see internal credit operations.

This creates an unapplied CreditMemo. It does not apply credit to an invoice, create a Payment or RefundReceipt, repay cash, change native stock, or record a second native credit. Those actions require separate reconciliation. ## Managed credential key rotation

Rotation changes the application encryption key for the entire integration credential vault in one database; it does not rotate QuickBooks tokens or the client secret. It requires no provider request and changes no stock, order, invoice or cash facts. Use separate regional databases and procedures. Before the first migration/rotation, stop and upgrade **all** API, worker and operator processes to the version implementing the key-generation checks. Older binaries are not fenced by the new marker. Keep provider access disabled during this local procedure.

Export `DATABASE_PATH`, `DATA_REGION` and the **current** `PROVIDER_ENCRYPTION_KEY` through protected runtime configuration. `npm run provider:credentials -- key-status` returns only `{generation,configured,current}`. Generation zero means no marker exists yet; `current:true` at generation zero only reports configured-key availability. It does not prove legacy ciphertext can be decrypted. Installation, authorization and rotation verify all existing legacy material before accepting that key. A first installation registers generation one in the same transaction as the ciphertext write.

Use `npm run provider:credentials -- rotate` with protected noninteractive stdin, capped at 32 KiB. Input shape is `{generation,nextKey,workers:[{orgId,workerUserId}]}`: the inspected generation, a different independently generated 64-character hexadecimal key, and one current active finance worker for **each** organization with credentials or pending/exchanging authorization. No secrets belong in command arguments, history, repository files or logs. The command accepts at most 100 distinct organization authorities and 1,000 bindings. These are functional bounds, not qualified production capacity; initial legacy verification still scans all encrypted material before the rotation bound is checked.

Inspect metadata with `npm run provider:credentials -- key-status`; the result is `{generation,configured,current}`. Generation zero means there is no persisted marker yet; `current` reports configured-key/marker compatibility, not independent proof that legacy ciphertext decrypts. Run `npm run provider:credentials -- rotate` with protected stdin containing `{generation,nextKey,workers:[{orgId,workerUserId}]}`. Use the inspected generation, the new key as `nextKey` and all affected organization workers. No binding variables or client secret are required for these two vault commands. The first encrypted installation registers generation one atomically; a legacy vault can rotate from generation zero after proving its material.

Rotation refuses a restore hold, revoked/password-restricted worker, missing organization authority, stale generation, corrupt ciphertext or material/state mismatch. It also refuses any active or abandoned refresh claim or exchanging OAuth attempt. Inspect binding/attempt status first. Let active exchanges finish; resolve interrupted refresh credentials through the existing revision-checked disable/reconnection procedure, and canceled/unknown authorization through the authorization procedure. Never silently discard a claim and resend an already transmitted operation. Canceling or disabling locally does not revoke upstream tokens.

Inside one exclusive write transaction, rotation decrypts every ready binding, re-encrypts it with a new nonce and next revision, advances disabled/unknown revisions without reviving material, cancels pending OAuth attempts, advances the database key generation and records metadata-only audits for the supplied finance workers. Original token lifetimes and hard deadlines are preserved. Any failure rolls back the entire operation. The runtime replaces its in-memory key only after commit. The result is `{generation,bindings,canceledAuthorizations}`; it contains no credentials or key fingerprint.

After success, distribute the replacement key through the separately qualified key-custody mechanism and restart every API/worker/operator process. Verify `key-status` with the replacement reports the new generation and `current:true`, then inspect binding status. Processes running this version with an old key or old generation cannot install, refresh or begin/complete authorization. Even reusing an earlier key does not revive an already running stale generation; operators should still generate a fresh key for every rotation. Status, disable and authorization cancellation remain available for incident resolution under their current identity controls.

If the reply is lost, inspect key status with the replacement and current keys before retrying. The expected generation prevents blind replay; this command has no separate replay receipt. Do not downgrade the generation, delete its marker or overwrite credentials to force a retry. No lost-key reset or automatic rollback/distribution is implemented. The schema marker remains after restore while restored credentials are disabled; the restored generation still requires its matching configured key for future installation, alongside the provider hold/recovery controls. Old encrypted material may remain in SQLite journals and backups; rotation does not securely erase them.
