# Order quantity amendments

Local behavior under construction against D-019/D-020/D-021; all product gates remain NOT VERIFIED. This provisional commercial policy needs owner/customer/warehouse acceptance.

An administrator, commercial user or buyer for the order's account may amend the total ordered quantity of an existing line on an open order. The request names the current order revision, line, new total quantity, explicit backorder choice and a reason. It cannot change the product, warehouse, accepted description, currency, unit price or unit tax. The original accepted quote remains intact. Additional units use the original accepted unit price/tax even after catalog pricing changes; qualify that commercial policy before production use.

The quantity must change, stay between 1 and 100,000, and include every shipped or canceled unit. Use cancellation for a complete unsupplied-line cancellation. Closed orders cannot be reopened by amendment. A reduction removes unsupplied backorders first, then releases reserved stock. Picked or packed commitments are preserved when sufficient unpicked reservations can cover a reduction. If the reduction requires removing picked units, those units must be unpicked; packed stock must first have its packing voided through fulfillment. Reducing quantity changes the amended order total and outstanding order exposure; it does not reverse shipment, invoice, cash, refund or physical custody history.

An increase checks the current product status, customer hold, reservation deadline and total credit exposure, then reserves additional available stock at the order's warehouse. Without explicit backorder approval, insufficient additional stock rejects the entire amendment. Existing backorders remain for the Allocate action; adding quantity does not silently fulfill them. Current holds and due deadlines block increases, including zero-price lines. Reductions remain available while an account is held or its deadline is due.

Order lines, amended total, credit exposure, reservations, immutable amendment record, event, audit and idempotency receipt commit in one native database transaction. Concurrent expected-revision commands permit one change; a retry with the same actor/key/payload returns the original result. Changing inputs requires a new request key. Current actual user, organization, role, password and account/site authority are checked before history and cached command results. An old receipt cannot restore revoked access or replay stock changes.

Order history is a scoped descending sequence of pages of 20. Each record retains the old/new quantity, accepted unit price/tax, old/new order total, allocation delta, reason, actor and time. Reasons are visible to authorized order readers, including the buyer; do not put private internal notes there. Current order status and historical amendments serve different purposes. Refreshing or leaving the page cancels history reads; a failed continuation retains prior rows for retry.

See the separate [reservation deadline workflow](RESERVATIONS.md) for reviewed expiry and renewal. This quantity workflow does not provide product substitution, price renegotiation, warehouse reassignment, order-wide approvals or customer notifications. It does not prove actual customer agreement, printed custody, provider processing or production migration/load/security/recovery.
